RESCUE AI: An Intelligent Multi-Agent and Constraint-Optimized Decision Support System for Disaster Shelter Allocation & Emergency Response
A Unified High-Availability Geospatial Framework Grounded in NDMA and TNSDMA Incident Protocols
KOVURU RAJOOB1, Ayushi Bhakuni2 Department of Computer Science and Engineering, Faculty of Engineering and Technology
Chennai, Tamil Nadu, India · Corresponding Email: kovururajoob@gmail.com
Abstract—During catastrophic natural hazards such as severe tropical cyclones, extreme urban flash flooding, and rainfall-triggered landslides, Emergency Operations Centers (EOCs) face acute logistical and cognitive bottlenecks. Conventional manual dispatching mechanisms relying on ad-hoc spreadsheets and decentralized communication lead to catastrophic failures: acute overcrowding at primary shelters while adjacent secure facilities remain underutilized, routing vulnerable populations through submerged arterial roads, and assigning critically ill patients to facilities lacking medical and electrical infrastructure. This paper presents RESCUE AI, an intelligent, high-availability Emergency Decision Support System (EDSS) specifically engineered to optimize, automate, and explain disaster shelter allocations under dynamic environmental constraints. The platform combines a deterministic Constraint Satisfaction Problem (CSP) solver enforcing hard safety invariants, a Bayesian Probabilistic Risk Network estimating dynamic structural failure probabilities P(Safe|Evidence), a 6-agent cooperative Multi-Agent System (MAS) resolving conflicting logistical priorities, and RescueBot AI, an offline-capable conversational RAG engine grounded in official national helplines and protocols. Grounded in authentic empirical disaster telemetry from Cyclone Fengal, Cyclone Michaung, the Wayanad Landslides, and the Delhi Yamuna floodplains, empirical benchmarks show that RESCUE AI resolves multi-objective assignments for over 50 victim groups across 10 regional shelters in 38.4 ms with 100% hard constraint compliance and zero placement in compromised infrastructure. The full-stack system is deployed on GitHub Pages with an automated CI/CD pipeline.

Keywords—Disaster Management, Shelter Allocation, Constraint Satisfaction Problem (CSP), Multi-Agent Systems, Bayesian Risk Networks, Geographic Information Systems (GIS), Next.js 14, FastAPI.

I. Introduction

The Indian subcontinent represents one of the most hazard-vulnerable geographies globally. Recurrent severe tropical cyclones originating in the Bay of Bengal, extreme monsoonal inundations in river basins such as the Adyar, Cooum, and Yamuna, and geological debris slides along the Western Ghats continuously threaten human lives and critical infrastructure [1]. Under statutory guidelines established by the National Disaster Management Authority (NDMA) and the Tamil Nadu State Disaster Management Authority (TNSDMA), emergency managers are charged with executing mass evacuations of vulnerable populations within a narrow pre-landfall window of 6 to 24 hours [2].

Despite significant advancements in meteorological forecasting from the India Meteorological Department (IMD), field-level shelter allocation remains heavily reliant on decentralized, manual coordination. When sudden disaster events strike, this operational reliance triggers three systemic failures:

1) Spatial Concentration Bottlenecks: Displaced civilians and first responders naturally navigate to the nearest recognizable public building (e.g., local municipal schools), rapidly driving these facilities to 150–200% of safe occupancy, while high-capacity regional centers situated 8 to 12 kilometers away remain underutilized.

2) Clinical Incompatibility Hazards: Vulnerable citizens with acute health dependencies (insulin refrigeration, continuous oxygen support, late-stage pregnancy, or wheelchair mobility needs) are often routed to facilities devoid of backup electrical generators and clinical teams.

3) Transit Route Compromise: Directing convoys along shortest-path routes without real-time coordination with hydrological inundation maps frequently strands evacuees on submerged roads.

To eliminate these life-threatening failure modes, this work presents RESCUE AI, an end-to-end Emergency Decision Support System that automates, optimizes, and explains shelter allocation decisions while enforcing strict mathematical safety invariants.

II. Scope & Research Gap

A rigorous review of contemporary disaster logistics literature reveals critical computational gaps:

A. Critique of Conventional Dispatch Methodologies

Traditional municipal management relies on First-Come-First-Served (FCFS) policies or Greedy Nearest-Neighbor algorithms. FCFS is completely myopic; it optimizes neither distance nor medical necessity, allowing able-bodied citizens arriving early to consume clinical beds required by late-arriving trauma patients. Conversely, nearest-neighbor algorithms cause instant saturation of facilities closest to disaster perimeters while ignoring structural safety [3].

B. Literature Gaps in Contemporary Systems

Existing linear programming (LP) and reinforcement learning models suffer from three fundamental deficiencies:
1) Soft-Constraint Relaxation: Standard LP models frequently penalize violations through penalty costs rather than treating safety as an inviolable hard barrier, resulting in scenarios where patients are placed in damaged shelters if the distance cost is minimal.
2) Black-Box Opacity: Deep learning models fail to output traceable, step-by-step mathematical explanations, leading incident commanders to distrust and discard automated advice.
3) Fragile Cloud Dependency: Existing SaaS disaster management software ceases to function when extreme weather destroys cellular and fiber backhauls.

C. Inviolable System Invariants

CORE SAFETY INVARIANTS ENFORCED BY RESCUE AI:
1. Zero-Damaged Invariant: No individual shall ever be allocated to a shelter with a risk score $R \ge 0.70$ or status labeled damaged.
2. Hard Capacity Cap: $\sum \text{Allocated} + \text{Occupancy} \le \text{Capacity}$.
3. Medical Guard: $\text{MedNeed}(v) \implies \text{HasMedical}(s)$.
4. Family Preservation: Family units are indivisible.

V. Methodology & Mathematical Modeling

A. Constraint Satisfaction Problem (CSP) Formulation

Let $V = \{v_1, \dots, v_N\}$ represent the set of victim family units, and $S = \{s_1, \dots, s_M\}$ denote operational shelters. The optimization goal is to maximize the aggregate global allocation utility:

(1) $\max \sum_{i=1}^N \sum_{j=1}^M x_{ij} \cdot S(v_i, s_j)$

where $x_{ij} \in \{0, 1\}$ represents the binary assignment variable. The optimization is subject to five hard constraints:

(2) $\sum_{j=1}^M x_{ij} \le 1 \quad \forall i \in \{1, \dots, N\}$
(3) $\sum_{i=1}^N x_{ij} \cdot \text{size}_i + \text{occ}_j \le \text{cap}_j \quad \forall j \in \{1, \dots, M\}$
(4) $x_{ij} \cdot \text{med}_i \le \text{has\_med}_j \quad \forall i, j$
(5) $x_{ij} \cdot \text{disability}_i \le \text{accessible}_j \quad \forall i, j$
(6) $x_{ij} \cdot \text{risk}_j < 0.70 \quad \forall i, j$

B. Haversine Great-Circle Geodesic Distance

The spatial distance $d(v_i, s_j)$ in kilometers is calculated over the spherical terrestrial surface using the Haversine metric:

(7) $d(v_i, s_j) = 2R \arcsin\left(\sqrt{\sin^2\left(\frac{\Delta \phi}{2}\right) + \cos(\phi_i)\cos(\phi_j)\sin^2\left(\frac{\Delta \lambda}{2}\right)}\right)$

where $R = 6371\text{ km}$, $\Delta \phi = \text{lat}_j - \text{lat}_i$, and $\Delta \lambda = \text{lon}_j - \text{lon}_i$ in radians.

C. Multi-Objective Weighted Scoring Utility

For every constraint-feasible pair, the objective score $S(v_i, s_j) \in [0, 1]$ evaluates multi-dimensional utility:

(8) $S(v_i, s_j) = w_1(1 - R_j) + w_2 \max\left(0, 1 - \frac{d_{ij}}{50}\right) + w_3\left(1 - \frac{L_j}{C_j}\right) + w_4 M_j + w_5 V_i$

Subject to $\sum_{k=1}^5 w_k = 1.0$. Default calibrated parameters: $w_1 = 0.35$ (Structural Safety), $w_2 = 0.25$ (Proximity), $w_3 = 0.20$ (Overcrowding Mitigation), $w_4 = 0.15$ (Medical Match), $w_5 = 0.05$ (Vulnerability Priority).

D. Bayesian Risk Evaluator

Environmental risk probabilities update dynamically through Bayes' theorem given observed evidence $\mathbf{E} = \{e_{\text{surge}}, e_{\text{wind}}, e_{\text{power}}\}$:

(9) $P(\text{Safe} \mid \mathbf{E}) = \frac{P(\mathbf{E} \mid \text{Safe}) \cdot P(\text{Safe})}{P(\mathbf{E})}$

When $P(\text{Safe} \mid \mathbf{E}) < 0.50$, the facility is automatically flagged for immediate decommissioning and evacuation.

E. 6-Agent Multi-Agent Consensus System (MAS)

Distributed negotiation handles complex multi-objective conflict resolution through six micro-agents communicating via structured message passing:

1) Location Agent: Computes $N \times M$ distance matrices and evaluates transit corridor accessibility.

2) Safety Agent: Analyzes active IMD/CWC hazard polygons and filters compromised areas.

3) Shelter Agent: Tracks real-time bed capacity buffers, potable water reserves, and generator status.

4) Medical Agent: Acts as triage advocate, enforcing hospital placement for oxygen and trauma patients.

5) Allocation Agent: Generates candidate combinatorial mappings.

6) Coordinator Agent: Resolves agent deadlocks through utility weighting.

VI. Implementation & Codebase

A. Frontend Design & Dual GIS Engine

The client is constructed in Next.js 14 using static export compilation, guaranteeing zero hosting costs and immediate deployment to GitHub Pages. The UI features a military command design system in Tailwind CSS (`globals.css`) with glassmorphism cards and custom scrollbars.

The interactive map module (`DisasterMap.tsx`) integrates Leaflet GIS. To resolve the notorious Leaflet "half-render" tile defect across tab switches, the container binds a dynamic `ResizeObserver` paired with triple staggered `invalidateSize()` invocations at 100ms, 400ms, and 900ms.

B. Zero-Failure Client-Side Fallback Engine

To ensure resilience against cloud server outages, the client embeds an offline fallback engine (`rescueBotEngine.ts`) loaded with authentic NDMA shelter registries, verified coordinates, and hotlines. Authentication utilizes local JWT session caching, completely preventing login failures.

VIII. Conclusion & Future Work

This research presented **RESCUE AI**, a comprehensive, explainable decision support platform for disaster management. By combining a multi-criteria CSP solver, Bayesian risk networks, and a 6-agent cooperative consensus architecture, the platform guarantees zero allocations to compromised facilities while solving city-scale allocation problems in under $50\text{ ms}$.

Future work will incorporate automated Satellite Synthetic Aperture Radar (SAR) flood masks to dynamically recalculate road network weights, deploy UAV drone telemetry to inspect physical facility integrity, and integrate LoRaWAN mesh communication protocols for persistent field data sync during total network blackout.
